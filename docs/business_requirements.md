# ShopSense — Online Purchase Intention Prediction

## 1. Business Requirements

### 1.1 Business Problem

The company wants to identify website visitors who are likely to make a purchase during their current session, in order to use marketing and personalization resources more effectively.

The goal is not simply to predict purchases, but to use the predictions to support better business decisions.

---

### 1.2 Business Objectives

#### Primary objective

Improve the company's ability to identify high-purchase-intent visitors.

#### Potential business objectives

- Prioritize personalized offers for high-intent visitors.
- Optimize marketing resources.
- Trigger targeted promotions when appropriate.
- Reduce unnecessary targeting of visitors unlikely to purchase.
- Better understand visitor purchasing behavior.
- Potentially increase conversion rate and revenue.

---

### 1.3 System Users

The potential users of the system are:

- Marketing/CRM teams.
- E-commerce personalization systems.
- Business analysts.
- Other systems that need purchase-intent predictions.

**Note:** In a real company, the exact users must be confirmed with stakeholders.

---

### 1.4 What Are We Trying to Improve?

We want to improve the company's ability to identify visitors with a high probability of purchasing compared with its current approach.

The system should help the company make better targeting decisions while avoiding unnecessary marketing actions.

**Open questions for the business:**

- How are visitors currently targeted?
- Is the current approach rule-based or ML-based?
- What is the current conversion rate?
- What is the current targeting performance?
- What problem does the current solution have?

---

### 1.5 Cost of a Prediction / Cost of Errors

The cost of a prediction is mainly related to the consequences of incorrect predictions.

#### False Positive

The model predicts that a visitor is likely to purchase, but the visitor does not purchase.

Possible business costs:

- Unnecessary promotion or discount.
- Unnecessary marketing cost.
- Reduced profit margin.
- Unnecessary use of personalization resources.

#### False Negative

The model predicts that a visitor is unlikely to purchase, but the visitor actually purchases.

Possible business costs:

- Missed marketing opportunity.
- Missed personalization opportunity.
- Potential lost revenue.

**Important business question:**

Which is more costly for the company: unnecessarily targeting a visitor or missing a potential buyer?

The answer will influence the model's decision threshold and evaluation metrics.

# 2. Machine Learning Formulation

## 2.1 ML Problem Type

This is a:

**Supervised learning → Binary classification problem.**

We have historical sessions for which the purchase outcome is already known.

The model learns from these examples to predict the outcome of future sessions.

---

## 2.2 Prediction Unit

One observation/row represents:

> **One website visitor session.**

The model therefore makes a prediction at the **session level**, not at the customer, product, or transaction level.

---

## 2.3 Target Variable

Target:

`Revenue`

Definition:

- `Revenue = True / 1` → the session resulted in a purchase.
- `Revenue = False / 0` → the session did not result in a purchase.

The model should ultimately estimate:

**P(Revenue = 1 | session features)**

In other words:

> The probability that a session will result in a purchase given the available session information.

---

## 2.4 Input Features

The dataset contains several categories of features.

### Numerical features

Examples:

- `Administrative`
- `Administrative_Duration`
- `Informational`
- `Informational_Duration`
- `ProductRelated`
- `ProductRelated_Duration`
- `BounceRates`
- `ExitRates`
- `PageValues`
- `SpecialDay`

### Categorical features

Examples:

- `Month`
- `OperatingSystems`
- `Browser`
- `Region`
- `TrafficType`
- `VisitorType`

### Boolean features

- `Weekend`

### Target

- `Revenue`

---

## 2.5 Prediction-Time Information

Before using a feature, we must ask:

> **Would this information genuinely be available at the moment the prediction is made?**

This is critical because a feature that contains information generated after the purchase decision could create **data leakage**.

We therefore need to investigate features such as:

`PageValues`

before deciding whether they are valid prediction features.

---

## 2.6 Data Leakage

Potential leakage must be investigated before model training.

We need to determine:

- How each feature is calculated.
- When each feature becomes available.
- Whether it depends directly or indirectly on the target.
- Whether the feature would exist in a real-time production prediction scenario.

A highly predictive feature is not automatically a good feature.

If the feature would not be available at prediction time, it must not be used.

---

## 2.7 Evaluation Metrics

Because the positive class (purchase) is relatively uncommon, **accuracy should not be our primary metric**.

### Primary metrics

- Precision
- Recall
- PR-AUC

### Secondary metrics

- F1-score
- ROC-AUC
- Confusion matrix
- Probability calibration

The final threshold should not automatically be `0.5`.

It should be selected according to the business cost of false positives and false negatives.

---

## 2.8 Baseline

Before evaluating complex models, we should establish baselines.

Potential baselines:

1. Majority-class classifier.
2. Simple Logistic Regression.

The purpose is to determine whether more complex models actually provide meaningful improvement.

---

## 2.9 Candidate Models

We will initially compare:

- Logistic Regression
- Random Forest
- XGBoost

The final model will be selected based on:

- predictive performance
- business requirements
- interpretability
- inference speed
- complexity
- deployment requirements

We should not choose the most complex model simply because it produces the highest score.
